# The four pillars in practice

Each pillar has four dimensions. Each dimension carries **one probe** — the
question that surfaces it in conversation — and **one piece of evidence** —
what you observe when it is strong. Use the probe; watch for the evidence.

---

## G — Goal

*A destination precise enough that today has an obvious first move.*

**When it is the limiting pillar:** effort scatters. Work happens, but nothing
compounds toward anything.

| Dimension | Probe | Evidence when strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clarity of the target | "What score, by what date, and how far away are you right now?" | States all three without hesitating |
| Standard of excellence | "What would make you unwilling to submit a piece of work?" | Has a definition of *done well* stricter than *finished* |
| Decomposition | "Which single skill, if it improved this week, moves the score most?" | Names this week's sub-goal and why it beat the others |
| Commitment | "What have you already given up to protect this goal?" | The goal survives a bad week rather than being quietly revised down |

**Coaching note.** A student who cannot answer the first probe does not have a
goal problem to be solved by motivation. Set the target and the date first;
everything downstream depends on it.

---

## I — Inspirits

*The fuel that keeps working on the days no one is watching.*

**When it is the limiting pillar:** a strong start, then silence by week
three. Plans exist; nothing runs them.

| Dimension | Probe | Evidence when strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desire and drive | "What does reaching this open up that matters to you?" | Talks about the goal in the first person, unprompted, with specifics |
| Belief | "When a score disappoints you, what is the first thing you change?" | Treats a low score as information about method, not about worth |
| Nerve under pressure | "What happens to your thinking in the last five minutes of a module?" | Timed accuracy holds against untimed accuracy |
| Distinctiveness | "What kind of student are you becoming, regardless of the score?" | Knows what they bring that a peer with the same score does not |

**Coaching note.** This is the pillar most often mistaken for a character
problem. It is usually a meaning problem. Do not add method to a student whose
Inspirits are the constraint — more method makes it worse.

**Escalation.** Distinguish low Inspirits from distress. A student who has
stopped caring about a test is a coaching matter. A student who has stopped
caring about most things is not, and belongs with a qualified professional
immediately. Every practitioner level names this as a mandatory escalation.

---

## T — Talent

*The edge you already have, found and then deliberately sharpened.*

**When it is the limiting pillar:** time spread evenly across everything, so
nothing ever becomes excellent.

| Dimension | Probe | Evidence when strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known strengths | "Which question type do you want to see on test day?" | Names two skills where they beat their own average, with data |
| Quality of thinking | "Talk me through why B fails on that question." | Explains why a distractor is wrong, not only why the key is right |
| Speed and focus | "Where do you lose the most time — thinking or re-reading?" | Median time at or under target without losing accuracy |
| Leverage awareness | "If you had four hours left in total, what would you spend them on?" | Most study time goes to the few skills carrying the most score |

**Coaching note.** The quality-of-thinking probe is the highest-yield question
in the whole model. A student who can only say why the key is right has
memorised an answer. One who can say why B fails understands the item type.

---

## A — Action

*Habits that run whether or not you feel like it today.*

**When it is the limiting pillar:** understanding without accumulation.
Everything is known; nothing is fluent.

| Dimension | Probe | Evidence when strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consistency | "What time of day do you study, and what triggers you to start?" | Sessions happen on schedule without a reminder or a negotiation |
| Deliberate practice | "Was your last session comfortable? If so, it was probably wasted." | Works at the edge rather than repeating what is already easy |
| Closing the loop | "What did you get wrong last week, and have you seen it again since?" | Every wrong answer is understood and re-met later |
| Environment and team | "Who notices if you skip three days?" | Studies where starting is easy and quitting is visible to someone |

**Coaching note.** When Action is limiting, change exactly one thing: fix the
time and the place. Do not also change the material, the method, and the
schedule — you will not know which one worked.

---

## Reading a profile

1. **Find the limiting pillar.** The interface marks it. Start there.
2. **Check confidence before acting.** Below 35%, the profile is a starting
   hypothesis. Ask the probes rather than trusting the numbers.
3. **Read the drivers**, not just the score. The profile shows every signal
   that fed each pillar and its weight, so you can see *why* a pillar is low.
4. **Do one thing.** The interface deliberately produces a single next move.

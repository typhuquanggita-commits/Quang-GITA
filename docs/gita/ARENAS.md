# The three arenas

A method that only works at the desk is not a method. These are the places it
has to survive without the scaffolding.

Study is not one of them. Study is where the method is built.

---

# Family — opens at tier 2

**Purpose:** turn the home from a place that monitors studying into a place
that makes studying easy.

**Who has to act:** the learner, at least one parent or guardian, and siblings
where present. This arena cannot be delivered to a learner alone.

## Ritual: the household quiet hour — daily, 60 minutes

1. A fixed hour is agreed once and not renegotiated nightly.
2. Every person in the house works quietly — reading, paperwork, study.
3. No entertainment screens for anyone, adults included.
4. The hour ends on time even if the work is unfinished.

**Pitfall:** the adults exempt themselves. The hour then reads as a punishment
rather than a norm, and it dies within two weeks.

## Ritual: fifteen minutes on the numbers — weekly, 15 minutes

1. The learner opens their own analytics and presents it. The parent does not
   drive.
2. Three questions only: what improved, what did not, what changes next week.
3. The parent asks about **method**, never about effort or attitude.
4. The week ends with one specific commitment, written down.

**Pitfall:** it becomes an interrogation about effort. The learner then starts
managing the meeting instead of managing the work.

## Ritual: one honest sentence at the table — daily, 3 minutes

1. Each person names one thing that went well today and one that did not.
2. Adults go first, and are specific about their own failure.
3. Nobody fixes anybody. The sentence is heard, not solved.

**Pitfall:** an adult responds to the child's admission with advice. The
admissions stop the same week.

## Indicators

| Pillar | Indicator | Observable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action | A protected place and hour exist | Studies without negotiating for the space or the time |
| Inspirits | The household talks about method, not effort | A bad score prompts "what will you change?" rather than "you did not try" |
| Goal | The learner owns the goal out loud | The learner states the target to the family, rather than the family stating it to them |

# School — opens at tier 3

**Purpose:** convert classroom hours from time served into deliberate
practice, and use peers as an engine rather than a comparison.

**Who has to act:** the learner, subject teachers, and a study team of three
to five peers.

## Ritual: teach-back circle — weekly, 30 minutes

1. Each member teaches one concept for four minutes, without notes.
2. The group asks one hard question each — not a supportive one.
3. Anything the teacher could not answer becomes their drill for the week.

**Pitfall:** the group is kind. Soft questions make everyone feel fluent and
teach nobody anything.

## Ritual: the team board — weekly, 10 minutes

1. Post each member's weekly volume and adherence — **never** their score.
2. Celebrate the largest improvement, not the highest number.
3. A member who misses two weeks is asked what changed, not warned.

**Pitfall:** scores go on the board. The strongest student stops trying and
the weakest stops coming.

## Indicators

| Pillar | Indicator | Observable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspirits | Asks questions in class | At least one real question a week, in front of others |
| Action | Belongs to a working study team | The team has met three times this month without a teacher present |
| Talent | Can teach what they know | Has explained a concept to a peer this week and been questioned on it |

# Society — opens at tier 4

**Purpose:** prove the method is general — that it holds outside the subject
it was learned on — and turn ability into contribution.

**Who has to act:** the learner, a commitment outside school, and people who
benefit from it.

## Ritual: the second domain — weekly, 20 minutes

1. Choose one pursuit with nothing to do with the SAT — an instrument, a
   sport, a craft.
2. Apply exactly one GITA habit to it, unchanged.
3. Track adherence in that domain with the same honesty as study.
4. Report at the weekly coach session what transferred and what did not.

**Pitfall:** the second domain is chosen to be easy, so it proves nothing. It
should be something the learner has previously failed to sustain.

## Ritual: give the method away — monthly, 90 minutes

1. Find one person who wants what you can already do.
2. Teach them the smallest useful piece of it.
3. Ask afterwards what they will actually do differently.

**Pitfall:** it becomes a performance of generosity. If nobody changed
anything, it did not happen.

## Indicators

| Pillar | Indicator | Observable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action | A habit runs outside study | One GITA habit sustained for a month in a non-academic pursuit |
| Inspirits | Ability is used for someone else | Helped someone outside their circle using a strength, this month |
| Goal | The method is described as their own | Describes how they work without naming a coach, a class, or an app |

---

## Measuring transfer

Transfer per arena is the fraction of its indicators currently observed. It is
a fraction rather than a count so the three arenas — which have different
numbers of indicators — stay comparable on one chart.

Marking an indicator is a judgement, made by the learner and their coach
together. The phrasing is deliberately concrete so the judgement is about a
fact rather than an impression.

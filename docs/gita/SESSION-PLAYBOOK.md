# Running a GITA session

For coaches. Forty-five minutes, weekly.

## Before the session — 5 minutes

Open the learner's GITA profile and read three things:

1. **The limiting pillar.** This is the session's subject. Not the four
   pillars — the one.
2. **The confidence figure.** Below 35%, the profile is a hypothesis. Ask the
   probes rather than presenting numbers as findings.
3. **Habit adherence over 28 days.** Not the streak. Adherence is the honest
   number; a streak of four can sit on top of a month of misses.

Check the tier against your own certified level. If the learner is above it,
stop and hand the session on. That is the correct outcome, not a failure.

## Opening — 5 minutes

Ask what happened since last week. Do not ask whether they studied — the data
already answers that, and asking makes the session an interrogation.

Ask instead: **"What got in the way?"**

The answer is the session's real content more often than anything you planned.

## The body — 25 minutes

Work **only** the limiting pillar. The other three can wait, and that is the
whole point of naming one.

Use the probes from [PILLARS.md](PILLARS.md). One probe, then listen. The
common failure here is asking a probe and answering it yourself.

By pillar:

**Goal limiting.** Get the target and the date fixed before anything else.
Then get them to name the one skill this week is for, and to say why it beat
the alternatives. If they cannot, the decomposition is the work.

**Inspirits limiting.** Do not add method. A student whose fuel is the
constraint experiences more method as more weight. Work on what reaching this
opens up, and be specific — "a good university" is not an answer, it is a
category. Watch for distress that is not about studying; escalate immediately
if you see it.

**Talent limiting.** Ask them to explain why a distractor is wrong on a
question they got right. This is the highest-yield question in the model. A
student who can only say why the key is right has memorised an answer.

**Action limiting.** Change exactly one thing: the time and the place. Do not
also change material, method, and schedule — you will not know which worked,
and neither will they.

## Closing — 10 minutes

Three things, in order:

1. **One commitment, written by them.** Specific enough to be answered yes or
   no next week. "Study more" is not a commitment. "Thirty minutes at 7pm at
   the kitchen table, Monday to Friday" is.
2. **One habit**, taken on or dropped. Never more than one change at a time,
   and never above five active habits.
3. **What they will tell their family.** For tier 2 and above. If the home
   does not know what changed, the home cannot support it.

## After the session — 2 minutes

Update the transfer board if an indicator changed. Note anything you escalated.

## Things that make a session fail

- **Working more than one pillar.** The learner leaves with four priorities,
  which is none.
- **Presenting a low-confidence profile as fact.** They will either dispute it
  or, worse, believe it.
- **Asking about effort instead of method.** "You need to try harder" is not
  actionable and is usually not true.
- **Fixing it yourself.** A commitment you wrote is one they will negotiate. A
  commitment they wrote is one they will keep.
- **Skipping the escalation.** If something in the session was outside your
  level or outside coaching altogether, that is the finding. Act on it.

# For families

## What this asks of you

Three rituals and one change in how you talk about school. That is the whole
ask.

You do not need to understand the SAT, the scoring, or the analytics. You need
to make the house a place where studying is easy, and to talk about **method**
instead of **effort**.

## The one change: method, not effort

When a score disappoints, the natural question is "did you study enough?"

It is the wrong question, for a specific reason: it can only be answered with
a defence. Your child will either defend themselves or agree they failed, and
neither response changes anything next week.

Ask instead: **"What will you change?"**

That question has an answer. It puts your child in the position of diagnosing
their own work, which is the skill the whole model is trying to build.

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "Did you study today?" | "What did you work on today?" |
| "You're not trying hard enough." | "What's getting in the way?" |
| "Why is this score lower?" | "What will you change this week?" |
| "Your friend got a higher score." | *Nothing. Say nothing.* |

## Ritual 1: the household quiet hour — daily, one hour

Agree a fixed hour once. Do not renegotiate it every evening — the
renegotiation is the thing that kills it.

During that hour, **everyone** in the house works quietly. Reading, paperwork,
study. No entertainment screens, and that includes you.

**This is the part that matters, and it is the part most families skip.** A
child studying alone while the rest of the house relaxes is fighting the room,
not the material. If the adults exempt themselves, the hour becomes a
punishment, and punishments get resisted.

End on time even when the work is unfinished. An hour that runs long teaches
your child that starting is dangerous.

## Ritual 2: fifteen minutes on the numbers — weekly

Sit with your child while **they** open their own analytics and present it.
You do not drive. You do not scroll.

Three questions, and only three:

1. What improved?
2. What did not?
3. What changes next week?

Ask about method. Never about effort or attitude. The moment this becomes an
interrogation, your child starts managing the meeting instead of managing the
work — and you lose your only honest window into how it is going.

End with one specific commitment, written down. Written by them.

## Ritual 3: one honest sentence at the table — daily, three minutes

At the evening meal, each person names one thing that went well today and one
that did not.

**Adults go first, and are specific about their own failure.** "Work was fine"
is not participating. "I put off a call I should have made this morning" is.

Nobody fixes anybody. The sentence is heard, not solved.

This is the hardest rule in the handbook and the most important. The first
time you respond to your child's admission with advice, the admissions stop —
that week, not eventually. A child who can name a bad day out loud stops
hiding bad weeks, and a hidden bad week is how a term goes wrong before anyone
notices.

## What you should not do

- **Do not set the goal.** A goal your child states to you is one they own. A
  goal you state to them is one they comply with, and compliance does not
  survive week three.
- **Do not compare them to anyone.** Not a sibling, not a classmate, not
  yourself at their age.
- **Do not add study time as a punishment.** It teaches that studying is what
  happens when you have done something wrong.
- **Do not check the app behind their back.** The weekly fifteen minutes is
  the window. Widening it without agreement costs you the window.

## When to raise something with the coach

- Your child has stopped talking about the goal entirely.
- A change at home has affected the routine — illness, a move, a family
  situation.
- The quiet hour is failing and you cannot see why.

## When to go past the coach

If you are seeing withdrawal, sleep changes, or distress that extends beyond
school, that is not a study problem and a coach is not the right person.
Contact a qualified professional. Every practitioner in this programme is
instructed to escalate exactly this, immediately, and you should too.
